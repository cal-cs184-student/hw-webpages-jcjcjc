Part 2: Simulation via numerical integration

We experimented with some parameters of the simulation.

Spring constant

With a low ks, the cloth sags and stretches more since the springs are weaker. We see more rippling and wrinkling, and the cloth is generally more "bouncy". With a high ks, the cloth looks much stiffer and tighter. It holds its shape better and does not droop as much. The difference occurs and is most visible at the end of the simulation, when the low ks cloth sags mostly in middle of the cloth between the two pinned corners.

Density

Lower density makes the cloth feel tighter and more lightweight. Higher density causes more sagging and stretching, similar to what low ks looks like. The difference occurs and is most visible at the end of the simulation, when the high density cloth sags mostly in middle of the cloth between the two pinned corners.

Damping

With low damping, the cloth swings and flops around a lot before coming to rest, with lots of wrinkles and movement. It looks as though there is very little air resistance. With high damping, the cloth falls slowly and smoothly as one piece, barely flopping around at all. The difference occurs right when starting the simulation. Low damping causes the cloth to swing back and forth for a long time, while high damping barely lets it swing at all.

Part 3: Handling collisions with other objects

Sphere collisions: For each pointmass in the cloth, we check if it is inside the sphere by comparing its distance from the sphere's origin to the radius. If yes, then we compute the tangent point by extending the vector from the origin to the pointmass out to the sphere's surface. We then compute a correction vector from last_position to this tangent point and apply it scaled by some value for friction.

Plane collisions: For each pointmass in the cloth, we check if it has crossed the plane by comparing the sign of the dot product of (position - point) and (last_position - point) with the plane normal. If the signs are not the same, it crossed. We find the tangent point by projecting the current position onto the plane. The correction vector from last_position to this offset point (also scaled for friction) is applied.

Here is our shaded cloth from scene/sphere.json in its final resting state on the sphere with different spring constant values. With a low ks, the cloth drapes loosely over the sphere, closely mirroring the spherical shape, almost hugging the sphere. With the default ks = 5000, the cloth sits on top, slightly more rigidly. With a high ks, the cloth is very stiff and juts out over sphere rather than draping down.
